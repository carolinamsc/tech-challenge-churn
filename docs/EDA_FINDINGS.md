# EDA — findings and decisions

> This document records decisions supported by the dataset inspection. Numeric results that depend on executing the local notebook should be generated by `notebooks/01_eda_baseline.ipynb`, rather than hard-coded here.

## Dataset

The project uses the IBM/Kaggle **Telco Customer Churn** dataset. The published dataset contains 7,043 customer records and 21 columns, with `Churn` as the target variable.

Source: https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/blastchar/telco-customer-churn

## Initial observations

- `customerID` is an identifier and should not be used as a predictive feature.
- `SeniorCitizen` is stored as an integer indicator; its treatment should be decided explicitly rather than assuming it is continuous.
- `tenure` is numerical and has direct business meaning as customer lifetime in months.
- `MonthlyCharges` is numerical.
- `TotalCharges` is delivered as an object/string in the canonical CSV because some records contain blank values. Literature describing this exact dataset reports 11 missing values in `TotalCharges`.
- `Churn` is binary and moderately imbalanced: published analyses report approximately 26.5% churn.

## Missing `TotalCharges`

We will not blindly drop the affected rows or replace blanks with zero before inspecting them.

The notebook will:

1. convert `TotalCharges` with `pd.to_numeric(errors="coerce")`;
2. identify the affected customers;
3. compare their `tenure`, `MonthlyCharges` and `Churn` with the full population;
4. document the final treatment.

This matters because a blank `TotalCharges` is structurally different from a genuine zero charge: these records are associated with customers at the beginning of their tenure.

## Leakage policy

The following rules will be applied before model training:

- no target-derived features;
- no use of `customerID`;
- preprocessing is fitted only on training folds through an sklearn `Pipeline`/`ColumnTransformer`;
- the test set remains untouched until final evaluation;
- cross-validation is performed only on the training portion.

## Modeling hypotheses to test

These are hypotheses, not conclusions:

1. Contract type is likely to be strongly associated with churn.
2. Short-tenure customers may have higher churn risk.
3. Payment method may contain useful behavioral signal.
4. Monthly charges may interact with contract and service configuration.
5. Demographic variables such as gender should not be assumed predictive without evidence.

## Next modeling step

After the EDA is executed, the preprocessing pipeline will be defined and compared using:

- Logistic Regression as the baseline;
- Random Forest;
- `MLPClassifier` from scikit-learn;
- stratified cross-validation;
- ROC-AUC, F1, precision, recall and confusion matrix;
- a business-oriented metric/threshold analysis.

The final model will be selected from measured results, not from model complexity.
